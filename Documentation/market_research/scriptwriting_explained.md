# Scriptwriting Explained

For this project it is essential to understand the format and standards that go into a script. The
sources used for this research are in the [sources](#sources) list at the bottom of this file.

"A screenplay isn't a finished work of literature, but instructions for collaboration" (Celtx).
Roles that need to use the screenplay for differing purposes are:
- Director to visualise the shots.
- Producer to estimate the budget and schedule.
- Actors to understand the rhythm and emotional flow.
- Cinematographer who thinks in terms of frames and movement.
- Editor who imagines pacing before a scene is shot.

## Script Standards

All screenplays must be in 12-point Courier New font.

### Spec Script vs Shooting Script
A spec script will not have shooting script elements such as camera angles, editing directions and
technical instructions. Scenes are also not numbered in a spec script but are in a shooting script.

### Standard Screenplay Formatting

The three parts of a screenplay are **Scene Headings**, **Narrative Description**, **Dialogue**.

**Scene Headings**
Master Scene Headings consist of:
- Camera location (EXT. or INT.)
- Scene location
- Time (DAY or NIGHT)

There are also **secondary scene headings** and **special scene headings** for flashbacks, dreams,
montages, etc. 

**Narrative Description (Action)**
- Action
- Setting and character (visual images)
- Sounds

**Dialogue**
- Character Cue (the name of the person speaking) always in **CAPS**
- Parenthetical or actor's direction. (optional)
- The speech

The script should be 8.5"x11" and the length should be about 90 to 120 pages depending on the
contents.

#### Title Page
The title should be in CAPS and can be optionally put in quotation marks. If there is more than one
writer then the names should be divided with `&`. If the word `and` is used it means that a writer
was brought in later to rewrite anothers script.

Information needs to be on the lower right corner. This could be the writers email address, phone
number or other information about the drafts (and **copyright**).

#### Typeface
Courier 12-point font must be used and is the standard. A `-` is used in master and secondary scene
headings. A `--` is used in narrative description and dialogue.

#### Margins
The left margin should be 1.5 inches, the right margin should be 1 - 1.25 inches. The top and bottom
margins should be 1 inch. 

10 characters is 1 inch??

The software should type **55 lines** per page.

- Left margin at 15 spaces (1.5 inches)
- Dialogue at 25 spaces (2.5 inches)
- Actors instructions at 31 spaces (3.1 inches)
- Character's name at 37 spaces (3.7 inches)

Dialogue shouldn't extend more than 65 spaces (6.5 inches) from the left edge of the page. Actors
instructions shouldn't be more than 50 - 55 spaces from the left edge of the page. Parentheticals
shouldn't be wider than 2 inches (20 characters.)

**This information should be checked**

**Page numbers** should appear in the upper right flush to the margin and have half an inch from the
top of the page, followed by a period. No page number should appear on the first page of the script.

**FADE IN:** is optional at the beginning of the script and if this is used in the future it should
be following a previous **FADE TO BLACK.**

It is also optional to have a **ROLL CREDITS**, **BEGIN CREDITS** and **END CREDITS**. This is
treated as a heading.

**FADE OUT** and **FADE TO BLACK** appear in the right margin.

If a scene is both outside and inside for the camera placement then **INT./EXT. CAR - DAY** is
standard.

Occasionally **SAME** is used to indicate that this scene takes place at the same time as the last
scene. **LATER** could also be used to indicate the passage of time.

Screenplays have the following elements:
- Scene Heading
- Action
- Character Cue
- Dialogue

A screenplay is comprised of a number of different scenes and every scene starts with a **Scene
Heading**. A scene heading contains three elements:
- Whether the scene takes place inside (INT.) or outside (EXT.)
- The location where the scene takes place. (House, Cafe, Beach)
- The time of day (DAY, NIGHT, MORNING, AFTERNOON, EVENING)


## Technical Standards

Once a script has been developed and is being sent round whether that be between the writer,
director, producer, actors; This is done using the `.pdf` format. This would mean that a 
screenwriting editor should have the ability to export to a `.pdf` as one of its core features. 
*This can be seen from the fact that competitions such as [these](https://www.oscars.org/nicholl/about#field-tabbed-content-tab-1)
only accept scripts in the pdf format.

During development of the script, the industry standard is to use the `.fdx` format. This is based
of the `.xml` format which stands for **eXtensible Markup Language**. XML does not use predefined
tags and is information wrapped in tags (W3Schools XML). This is useful as characters and scenes
now become nodes and are not just plaintext in an editor.

Where this differs with the `.fdx` format is that the elements are set and standardised  

[fountain.io](https://fountain.io/) is a plain text markup language and can be researched for its
appropriateness in this project.
